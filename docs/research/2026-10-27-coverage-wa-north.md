# October 27, 2026: Cowlitz, Lewis and Wahkiakum coverage audit

Delivered 41 complete new spot records and 99 inventory decisions. The inventory retains original WDFW facility names as aliases, so the next update can compare the official list directly against the map. Twenty-nine additions have an estimated drive range ending at 90 minutes or less after the boundary-route correction; four of those are inactive for a specific current reason. Outer-range candidates remain available up to 120 minutes.

## What was checked

The audit compared all WDFW county access pages and the agency's current geographic access inventory against the existing 95-record corpus. It then checked Cowlitz County's full parks inventory and 2024-2030 plan, Castle Rock and Longview park lists, Port of Kalama's fishing-access inventory, Toledo's launch information, Lewis County park/access information, Tacoma Power's public recreation sites, both Wahkiakum ports, the Lower Columbia Estuary Partnership launch inventory and FWS refuge fishing rules. Named resorts, informal launches, hatcheries and conservation properties were investigated where they could fill a real gap. Actual Esri satellite exports were downloaded and inspected for the populated candidates. Separate parking and bank coordinates were selected from those images. Tacoma Power's own Blue Creek aerial photo, Cowlitz County's Coweeman map and a ground photo of Coal Creek's launch were also inspected. Coordinates are labeled approximate and imagery-supported. They identify a supported shore approach, with water level and vegetation determining the dry standing position. Image capture dates were not supplied. Non-live OSRM estimates use northwest Vancouver at 45.7098, -122.7073. Agency points and additional park coordinates were routed separately. They are ordinary off-peak estimates, not current traffic times.

## Useful additions

The Kalama corridor now has Fisherman's Loop, Sportsman's Club, Modrow, Beginners' Hole, Hand, Prichard's, Red Barn and the Marine/Rasmussen public beach. The Cowlitz corridor gains Gerhart Gardens, Al Helenberg, Lions Pride, Cook Ferry, Hog Island, Olequa, I-5/Mandy, Wallace Bar, both Toledo accesses and the real Blue Creek public launch bank. Tower Bridge, Harry Gardner, Coweeman, Abernathy and additional Silver Lake access fill smaller-water gaps. Willow Grove and County Line supply long public Columbia beaches to the west. Cook Ferry is especially relevant to personal space: the county documents 2.5 miles of public river trail, three trailheads and fishing. Wallace Bar has a legally described 25-foot easement along nearly two miles of Cowlitz bank. Neither description means every foot is clear for casting.

## Current restrictions and findings

- **Beginners' Hole:** inactive for bank fishing's conventional tackle through October 31. The pipeline-to-Fallert intake reach is fly-only September 1-October 31. Review November 1. Hand and Prichard are farther upstream.
- **Coweeman and Abernathy:** fall closure through October 31; review November 1. Abernathy also requires selective gear, so conventional artificial lures can work but bait cannot.
- **Coal Creek Slough:** included inactive for dinner-fishing planning. Ecology's June 10 and July 2 updates say pH returned to normal and the emergency response ended, while continuing CDID advice says to avoid fishing in the ditch/slough system. The geographic reach of that continuing guidance at the county launch was not explicitly resolved. The record does not claim the public launch gate is closed or current chemical contamination remains. Columbia mainstem recreation explicitly remained open.
- **Mossyrock Park:** day-use land access stays active despite low-water boat-ramp closures. The ramp/dock notice is not treated as a whole-park closure.
- **Plummer:** the WDFW Lewis Street parking lot and restroom are closed, with walk-in access still allowed. The new entry uses the distinct city fishing parcel at Brick Wagner Park. Curb parking must obey signs and leave driveways clear.
- **Silver Lake, Cowlitz:** latest retrieved toxin record is June 23, 2025, below detection at Streeter's. No current local warning was found. That old result is not current clearance; the Whatcom County Silver Lake record was not substituted.
- **Blue Creek:** the owner confirms free public parking and river access at 273 Blue Creek Lane. The former erroneous private-gate directions were not reused. The selected bank is downstream from the launch and away from the closed outfall radius. The WDFW agency point alone refers primarily to ADA platforms, not a complete parking route.
- **Harry Gardner:** the park-side South Fork approach is included, with the installed weir exclusion described. The parent river and forks have different regulations. This pin identifies the supported bank approach; posted weir limits still control the casting position.
- **Barrier Dam:** current WDFW narrative says a 100-foot downstream boundary, but current codified text still says 400 feet. The selected bank lies farther downstream and avoids the discrepancy.

## Fishing evidence and ratings

The October 19-25 WDFW survey recorded 1 retained coho among 36 Cowlitz bank anglers below I-5, about 0.03 fish per surveyed angler. Above I-5 it recorded 2 coho jacks and 1 steelhead among 53 bank anglers, about 0.06 retained fish per surveyed angler. It does not report comparable fishing hours. The four sampled Kalama River bank anglers caught nothing; this is a very small sample. Columbia at Kalama recorded 1 coho among 6 bank anglers. The new Cowlitz entries preserve good general keeper-potential scores and use a temporary current-season score of 4 through November 3. Active Kalama entries use 5 through November 3 because the sample is too small for a stronger conclusion. These values are judgments, not facts about what any given bank will produce. They should be reassessed against the next survey, river conditions and an angler's reports. The recurring monthly profiles then resume automatically. Physical room is independent of traffic noise. Cook Ferry, Wallace Bar and long Columbia beaches score well for room. Compact ramp or dock entries receive lower bank and space scores while remaining visible. Managed trout lakes retain good potential without pretending a recent plant guarantees fish remain.

- **Green River/North Toutle Hatchery:** current public access across private timber roads is unresolved, with installed hatchery/weir closures. A hatchery facility address is not enough.
- **Germany Creek:** protected ownership confirmed; Land Trust policy requires site-specific public-access support, which was not found for the exact fishing route.
- **Mahaffey's and Camp Kalama:** campground fishing exists. Current day-only terms are unresolved for Mahaffey's; the Port specifically limits Camp Kalama's shore access to campers.
- **Ryderwood Pond, Silver Cove and Wildlife League access:** guest/public access or useful keeper evidence remains inadequate after the relevant owner/agency searches.
- **Julia Butler Hansen Refuge, creek-side Skamokawa ramp and Schaefer Park:** real access candidates at or beyond the 90-minute boundary, with unresolved precise parking/seasonal-route or rule-boundary details. These remain inventory follow-ups rather than invented pins.
- **Deep River and Mineral:** non-live routes are 130 and 139.7 minutes respectively, outside the 120-minute scope. This is a systematic expansion, with explicit remaining gaps. It is not a claim that every informal turnout or private day-access arrangement in three counties is resolved.
