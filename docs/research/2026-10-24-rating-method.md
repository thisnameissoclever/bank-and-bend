# Fishing scores assessed October 24, 2026

The overall score estimates an angler's general fit with a specific bank access during its regular favorable fishing season. Keeper potential carries 30% of the score. Physical personal space carries 12%, quiet setting 6%, manageable water 13%, casting quality 11%, bank quantity 7%, access ease 6%, scenery 5%, travel fit 5% and species fit 5%. Current-season prospects and research confidence are independent scores. Each remains visible, sortable and filterable. A seasonal score describes the dated trip opportunity; confidence describes the evidence behind the assessment. Missing recent reports alone do not reduce general keeper potential. A 7 represents a good fit, 8 an excellent fit with ordinary trade-offs, 9 an outstanding fit and 10 an exceptional match. The assessment uses reasonable judgment without requiring a quota of high scores. The retained physical constraint caps prevent good scenery or travel from offsetting very limited casting room, excessive current or poor personal space. Ambient traffic noise affects quietness. It does not establish that anglers cannot stand apart. Availability, legal retention and material advisories remain explicit trip conditions. The Washington and Oregon evidence notes record newly considered sources and independent assessments. For the remaining existing access points, the review used their dated agency fishery descriptions, stocking records, shore observations and an angler's firsthand accounts. It distinguished normal stocked-season potential from October holdover prospects. Several shallow urban ponds retain moderate October scores because their spring planting period has passed and warmwater-fish activity slows as temperatures fall. These are seasonal judgments, subject to revision with new observations. Each location retains its source URLs, checked dates and factor-specific reasons in data/spots.json. Future reviews must reconsider those reasons against new evidence and an angler's experience. Scores describe judgments rather than measured catch probabilities.
