# Oregon west and central coverage audit

Research date: October 27, 2026 Pacific. The current Marine Board access report displays October 28, 2026. Ratings are estimates for bank fishing and should be reconsidered as new evidence arrives.

## Deliverables

- `audit-or-west-spots.json`: 52 complete new records, 42 active and 10 seasonally inactive.
- `audit-or-west-inventory.json`: 239 decisions, including 96 added records or aliases, 23 existing or parallel-region entries, 88 exclusions, 20 outer-range entries and 12 deferred leads. These are inventory decisions, not 239 distinct fishing destinations.
- `audit-or-west-build.py`: reproducible regional generator. Do not use it to overwrite later owner reviews or changes. Root merges the records into the project corpus.

All 52 records have inspected aerial imagery and dated source links. Gales Creek and Joe Dancer explicitly retain a partial visual finding because canopy hides the final position. Scores are integers or null; overall rounding and owner weights remain the application's responsibility. No weights, filters, visits or favorites were changed by this audit.

## Coverage

The sweep cross-checked the complete regional Oregon Marine Board access inventory, the ODFW Portland access inventory, county and city parks, wildlife-area maps, river-access guides, current launch notices, Oregon fishing rules and OHA advisory information. Public launch margins were included when they provide a plausible lawful bank opportunity, even if they offer little room. Private moorages, pumpouts, boat-only islands and specific no-fishing facilities were distinguished from useful public accesses. Added coverage includes eleven Tualatin launches; four distinct Hagg Lake day-use banks; Fairview Lake's public platforms; five Columbia Slough launch records, with the two Elrod entries grouped; Smith and Bybee; Swan Island; Eastbank and Duckworth; Sauvie Island lake and channel accesses; Oregon Columbia beaches and ramps; Clatskanie, Beaver Landing and Westport; upper Nehalem parks and bridge access; Hawkins Park on Rock Creek; Gales Creek; Horning's; and the Yamhill parks. The south audit owns lower Tualatin sites downstream of Stark, Rogers Landing, Champoeg and the Willamette south of Sellwood. The east audit owns East Salish. These are explicitly resolved in the inventory rather than duplicated.

## Findings that change trip decisions

- Cook Park's launch parking is closed through mid-November, but the ramp and dock remain available on foot or by bicycle. The record uses upper park parking and a walk.
- Rood Bridge's floating dock was removed during the week of October 19. Its concrete ramp remains an access option.
- Hagg Lake is at a low pool, reported as 28 percent of capacity. Its public banks remain useful, but the waterline can be farther from the established paths. The park's several banks are separate places with different walks and space.
- Mayger's road-end ownership issue does not close the west ODFW public beach entrance. The county road-vacation evidence and current access guide support the public approach. The straight private road-end approach is excluded.
- Sauvie interior lakes are closed during the wildlife-area winter season. Gilbert's Multnomah Channel ramp exception does not open Little McNary Lake beside it. The new lake record is separate and inactive.
- Horning's conventional keeper fishing is closed until May 2027. Winter fly catch-and-release does not meet an angler's request. The record remains visible as inactive.
- Upper Nehalem and Rock Creek records are inactive during their September through November fishing closure. Hawkins has a public pool-side bank and seasonal trout evidence, so it is included with realistic space and fish-size limits.
- Elsner's historic access overlaps the Tualatin River National Wildlife Refuge, whose current rules prohibit fishing. Devils Lake Fork is a closed unlisted Wilson tributary. Scappoose Bay Marina prohibits fishing within the marina. These are legal exclusions rather than missing-pin deferrals.
- Fairview Lake's public fishing platforms are at Lakeshore Park on NE 217th Avenue. The surrounding residential shore is private. County evidence supports crappie, and the Columbia Slough one-fillet-meal-per-month guidance applies.
- The Swan Island ramp is a BES public recreational facility with adjacent bank access. The city's proposed property sale requires that access to continue. It should not be excluded merely because it is absent from the Parks Bureau's designated fishing list.
- The OHA map reports the Portland Willamette precaution lifted October 7 and the Yamhill advisory lifted September 2, 2026. Resident-fish consumption advice remains separate. A lifted bloom notice is not a current fish-tissue test.

## Evidence and scoring

Personal space measures room to stand and fish with separation after ordinary visitors. It is independent of traffic or industrial noise. Bank quantity counts usable public frontage, not total park acreage. Water manageability describes the selected margin and ordinary controllability of bait or light lures. A nearby main-channel current does not cause an automatic slow-and-fast label. Lower Tualatin entries emphasize crappie and seasonal coho. They do not present trout as a credible lower-river target. Small creek trout candidates are marked as weak for dinner-sized fish. Columbia and channel salmon scores reflect regional timing and bank access, not visible fish or assumed catches at the exact point. Some lake and bank candidates have strong fit scores because of their actual combination of space, access and prospects; no arbitrary ceiling was applied. Each record includes a 12-month seasonal profile and its explanation. Profiles are judgment-based baselines, separate from legal closures and advisories. They are not future guarantees or objective measurements. New source classes used in this update include current boating access notices, land-use decisions, road-vacation documents, the live OHA map, public-property sale conditions, accessibility field guides and independent river launch descriptions. Several generic fishing pages confused waterbodies or public access; those claims were not used as authority.

## Remaining coverage limits

Deferred entries remain explicit in the inventory. Webster Pond has a public seasonal launch but no adequate accepted-species dinner rationale after the checked sources. Huddleston is beyond the core 90-minute area and has an unresolved current construction status; an older work announcement was not treated as proof of a present closure. Small Yamhill creek parks and private lakes were not turned into fishing dots solely because a map shows water. Drive ranges are off-peak estimates using the parent's regional OSRM screen where available, with modest allowances. They are not live traffic promises. Some useful edge records have a range that crosses 90 minutes and may therefore be hidden by the app's conservative upper-bound filter.
