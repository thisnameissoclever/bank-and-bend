# Washington east coverage audit, October 27, 2026

Prepared 23 complete new location records, 67 named inventory decisions and one existing-record patch. The additions cover upper Washougal, Bonneville-area lakes, Corps shorelines, Home Valley, Drano, Stevenson, Bingen, White Salmon and the eastern edge near Lyle. The inventory also records aliases, closures, private or tribal access, unresolved navigation gaps and destinations beyond the present radius.

## Useful additions

- Four upper Washougal accesses: Three Mile, County Line, Steel Bridge and Big Eddy. The WDFW access inventory, official local recreation pages and aerial images support them. County Line is also known as Public Fishing Road; it is one access rather than two dots.
- Five lakes: Little Ash, Ashes, Tunnel, Greenleaf and Gillette. Gillette requires about 75-105 minutes of walking each way, which is shown independently from the drive.
- Hamilton Island has separate ramp-side and west-lot banks. Bonneville North Shore remains a distinct fast-current option with a lower manageability score. Its large bank is not confused with easy water.
- Home Valley beach, Wind River county launch, Drano county launch, Bob's Beach, Bingen Marina Park and Bingen Sailboard Park add real public shore or ramp-margin options.
- Northwestern Park, Big Cedars and Moss Creek add smaller-river options near the two-hour edge. Doug's Beach has explicit official smallmouth shore-fishing support and a controlled railway crossing.
- High Bridge on the Wind is stored inactive for dinner fishing during its fall release-only season. It has a May-June retention season. Boat ramps are included only with a supported adjacent bank or usable shore margin. Casting must yield to launch traffic. The two county ramps have conflicting single-car language on the county website: the current Boat Launch Rules section allows cars with a county launch permit, while an older fees footnote retains a disability-permit-only restriction. The records disclose that specific conflict instead of treating the launches as boat-only by default.

## Corrections that matter

Drano hatchery-road access can be active during the documented weekday 8 a.m.-3 p.m. visitor window. FWS explicitly permits entrance-road fishing. The patch provides the manual approach from SR 14, preserves the warning about private Chinook Drive and does not assume October weekend access. An automated routing error and unspecified weekend hours did not establish a blanket closure. The October 12-18 bank sample remains 0 retained fish among 39 anglers; the separate boat sample is 93 among 113 anglers. High Bridge access was initially described by a March 2026 washout notice. A later April 6 notice on American Whitewater's lower-river page says Skamania County temporarily repaired Old Detour Road and restored vehicle access. The report and record use the later notice. Fall release-only rules are the reason for current inactivity. Spring Creek Hatchery State Park's general fishing listing conflicts with the permanent quarter-mile fish-ladder closure in the actual water rule. The inspected park shore is too close to the ladder to support a fishing recommendation, so it is explicitly excluded. Nearby public shore outside the boundary was not established. Actual WDFW releases confirm Little Ash received 2,000 rainbows January 14 at 2.5 fish/lb and 3,000 April 1 at 2.7 fish/lb. Little White Salmon received 3,000 rainbows May 18 at 2.5 fish/lb. The actual 2026 query returned no Tunnel or Gillette release. Tunnel's generic WDFW stocking prose is therefore not treated as a fresh stocking report. Its documented perch population still supports inclusion. The county tourism claim of golden trout at Gillette was not adopted without corroboration.

## Research and limitations

The review used WDFW access GIS and waterbody inventories, county and port facility lists, Corps recreation descriptions, city shoreline planning maps, trail-association access records, actual stocking data, current WAC rules, emergency-rule searches, county health notices, the live state algae list, fish-consumption guidance and NAIP aerial imagery. New source types included the county shoreline ownership map, a spill-response public-launch inventory, American Whitewater access-repair notices and actual fish-release data. The current live algae page was opened directly. Search-index snippets retained older September lists, including Rowland, that differed from the live October list. Neither absence from that list nor a historical sample is described as current clearance. Mainstem additions above Fort Raines carry the Middle Columbia resident-fish consumption guidance separately from algae status. Migratory salmon and steelhead are distinguished from resident fish. Pins identify supported access areas. Tree-covered final paths at County Line, Steel Bridge, Little Ash, Tunnel, Greenleaf, Big Cedars and High Bridge are labeled partial visual verification. They are not represented as surveyed standing positions. Clearer bank and parking layouts have inspected imagery and matching operator access evidence. Aerial capture dates and current footing are not asserted. Travel ranges are off-peak estimates. The independent route screen found Three Mile 40.9 minutes, County Line 48.9, Steel Bridge 51.5, Drano county launch 93.8, Northwestern Park 108.6 and Doug's Beach 117.8. These support the retained ranges; eastern edge sites are not called nearby. Routing results for the regional screen are separate from exact parking verification. Moss Creek remains visible with empty precise pins and an unresolved off-season parking arrangement. Its federal listing supports the trout fishery; dense canopy and conflicting facility coordinates do not erase that evidence. No specific pedestrian fishing prohibition was found. It becomes inactive November 1 because the stream fishing season closes. Specific remaining gaps are preserved instead of hidden: a separate Rock Creek stream bank, safe Hamilton Creek bank footing, public approaches to low-elevation Blue/Little Blue, lower White Salmon private/tribal approaches and a specific public bank on the Lyle/Fisher Hill trail corridor. A seasonal campground closure was not treated as proof that lawful pedestrian fishing is prohibited. High Bridge provides one supported seasonal upper-Wind access; the historical survey's additional named entries remain in the inventory for section-boundary and navigation work. The audit is a substantial geographic expansion, not proof that every possible unnamed bank has been found. The checked official inventories are accounted for in the decisions file; several specific unresolved entries remain. Avoid presenting the inventory as complete until those entries have been resolved or explicitly accepted as follow-up work.
