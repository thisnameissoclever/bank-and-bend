# Fishing access expansion, October 27-28, 2026

The map grew from 95 to 322 distinct land-fishing access records. This update added 227 records: 90 in Washington and 137 in Oregon. A total of 255 records have a drive-range upper bound of 90 minutes or less: 111 Washington and 144 Oregon. With the default active-location and walking filters, 233 appear. Availability, species, personal minimums and saved state selections can reduce that count.

## Inventory review

The initial geographic screen covered 49 WDFW access facilities and 384 Oregon Marine Board entries. Of those 433 entries, 266 had a non-live road estimate of 90 minutes or less. These are screening records, including private marinas, duplicate launch services, islands and other facilities that do not independently qualify as fishing locations. Regional reviews also used county and city parks, utility properties, federal recreation lists, hatcheries, water trails, stocked-water lists and legitimate paid access. The maintained decision ledger has 742 entries, including aliases and repeated cross-region leads. That figure is not a count of unique fishing spots. Every added record has a corresponding inventory decision. A final unmatched-name pass resolved the remaining Snuffin Road label to the documented Eagle Creek take-out access hold. Separate banks on one river or lake remain separate when their parking, route, current, room or access rules differ. Nearby coordinates were checked for duplication. Cedar Creek and Lewis River Hatchery use opposite banks and separate roads. Little McNary's seasonal lake entry is distinct from the year-round Gilbert channel ramp. Launches, bank paths and fishing platforms are included with boating conflicts stated.

## Ratings and remaining questions

The scoring formula, owner-controlled importance values and physical-fit caps are unchanged. New factor judgments use the existing whole-number scale; weighted overall scores still display to one decimal. Monthly seasonal estimates can be fractional. Research confidence stays separate from fishing quality. Fourteen default-visible records score at least 8.0 under the baseline weights on the reviewed October date; custom weights can change that ranking. Confirmed public fisheries remain visible when canopy or conflicting maps prevent an honest precise parking or standing pin. Missing coordinates stay empty, with named directions and the specific limitation. Examples include Moss Creek, Marmot and several public park platforms. Lower-fit or compact launch banks receive actual trade-offs rather than being silently omitted. The ledger retains specific legal, physical-access and species questions, including private approaches, prohibited docks, fishway closures, and public trails without an established usable bank. It also retains outside-range candidates. A failed verification does not establish a closure. Future updates must reconsider these decisions and search additional sources rather than treating the written rationale as fact.
