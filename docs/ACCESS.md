## Required evidence

An active fishing recommendation requires legal parking, a continuous lawful route from parking, and a usable place on land to stand and fish. Confirm each part for the specified access point. Private ownership requires documented public access, an easement, or an applicable paid-access arrangement. A public road, river, boat ramp, nearby park, or agency point marker alone does not establish permission to use an adjoining bank. Use the land manager's maps and descriptions, official property boundaries, public access agreements, trail information, photographs and aerial imagery together. Parcel boundaries identify ownership; access rules and easements determine permission. An aerial image can show a clearing or path without establishing its legal status. Public trails can pass beside private houses, orchards and businesses. Check the actual route before judging it by appearance. Trace the route through any gates, crossings and shoreline approaches. Identify hours, seasonal restrictions, fees, railroad crossings, boat conflicts and physical obstacles. Never direct a bank angler across open water, through a private driveway, along a restricted railroad, or down an unsupported steep bank. Keep current closures separate from a location's normal public-access arrangements. Search for a supported alternative when the old coordinates fail. Retain the stable record ID when correcting the same access. Create a separate record for a materially different access point. Preserve the old coordinates and the reason for correction in dated audit evidence so future updates can detect a regression.

## Coordinate inspection

Inspect every parking and fishing coordinate against current available imagery and the source establishing access. Place parking pins within the supported parking area. Place fishing pins on usable land at the bank or on a permitted fishing platform. A waterbody center or midriver coordinate cannot represent a standing position. When using an ArcGIS image export, request the image metadata and retain its returned extent, coordinate system, width and height. The service can adjust the requested extent to fit the image. Convert image pixels using the returned extent and the correct projection. Check the corrected point on a new, centered image before accepting it. Never generate a bank coordinate by adding an arbitrary offset to a launch or waterbody point. Record the provider, inspection date, visible parking-to-bank relationship and relevant obstacles. State whether tree cover or image age limits the observation. A mapped public trail and a clearly identified bank can support an approach under trees. An undocumented path through a residential parcel requires further evidence.

## Application behavior

Each spot's `accessAudit` records a status, review date, summary, source IDs, individual findings, and separate parking, fishing-position and route checks. `supported` means the evidence establishes a legal approach and usable bank. `unresolved` means a material part remains unsupported after reasonable research. `blocked` means evidence establishes an access obstacle. Keep the specific finding in the summary. The effective availability calculation requires a supported access audit and route. A missing, unresolved or blocked audit makes the recommendation inactive even if a saved availability override says active. Such records remain searchable when inactive locations are included. Their ratings and personal records remain intact. The Parking and Fish here buttons require the corresponding supported coordinate, a supported route and active availability. Unsupported pins are null. Neither button falls back to a general-area point. Approximate supported points are labeled approximate. The General area button opens a map centered on the listing’s area coordinate at zoom 16. It has no parking or fishing marker. This prevents ambiguous place-name searches from resolving to another park. The area name remains visible in the corpus; the area coordinate is a browsing anchor, with no implication of permission to park or stand at that point. A known legal bank can remain in the research when its exact coordinate is withheld. Its audit must explain the supported approach and the remaining pin uncertainty. The corresponding coordinate button stays disabled. If an unresolved position leaves its bank current unknown, use an empty water-speed array and show a question mark; it does not match a selected current category. Current access and seasonal closures also disable coordinate directions, while supported pins remain available for map inspection.

## Updating an access review

Read the old audit and the current shared availability override. Reopen the cited sources and seek a relevant additional source. Recheck the route and any corrected pin instead of treating the last review as proof. Update only the fields actually reviewed; an access check does not refresh fishing rules, water advisories or stocking evidence. Correct the source record and publish it before restoring an availability override that was paused for a bad pin. Reopening a gate requires a current opening notice; correcting a coordinate requires evidence for the replacement route and bank. An owner availability edit cannot bypass an unresolved source audit. Run the access-navigation tests and the corpus checks. Inspect representative corrected and withheld locations in the interface. Verify that both the public research endpoint and connected research tools report effective availability and disabled directions consistently. Preserve saved filters, scoring settings, favorites, visits, comments and ratings.

## Required coverage and visible gaps

Every listed access requires visual inspection. Use satellite imagery and, where useful and available, Street View or other ground photographs to distinguish slopes from cliffs and identify entrances. Record sources, retrieval dates, visible facts and limitations. A partially obscured inspection remains an inspection. The Visually inspected filter uses that history independently of pin availability or current closure. The target is a supported fishing/shore-entry pin for every record, with a separate legal parking pin whenever attainable. Pursue manager maps, public boundaries, mapped paths and ground evidence before withholding a point because of canopy. Label supported approximate points honestly. A record without a pin must explain the specific missing fact in pinGaps.parking or pinGaps.fishing. These explanations appear beside unavailable navigation controls, including on touch screens. The availability reason separately explains a real closure, restriction or unresolved legal/physical approach. Do not label an actual open park closed merely because its exact bank coordinate is still missing. Retain inactivity when the continuous public route or usable bank materially lacks support after practical research. An inactive explanation must identify the affected access and obstacle in plain sentences. Distinguish seasonal fishing closure, park closure, retention restriction, private-property crossing, physical obstruction and coordinate uncertainty. Preserve the evidence date when only editing the wording. Source-backed reopening uses a fresh dated review and clears expired inactiveFrom dates through the revisioned availability writer.
